In an [earlier post](/blog/31/) I ended on a question I could not answer: the quantum prisoner's dilemma hands you a
ready-made institution, so is quantum game theory a way to _generate_ institutions for cooperation problems where we do
not already know the answer? The obvious next target was the tragedy of the commons.

There is a protocol for exactly that. Li, Du and Massar quantised the Cournot duopoly with squeezed light, and
Grau-Climent et al. carried their scheme over to a common-pool resource game. This post works out what that protocol
does, and the answer is disappointing in an interesting way. The entangler _is_ an institution — a clearing rule a
harbourmaster could run with a rulebook and a ledger. But it is the wrong institution for a commons, and no amount of
squeezing makes it the right one.

## 1. The fishery, run through the LDM protocol

Two islands fish a shared ground. The Li–Du–Massar (LDM) protocol [Li, Du, Massar, _Phys. Lett. A_ 306 (2002) 73]
defines, for each squeezing parameter $\gamma \ge 0$, a two-player game $Q_\gamma$ built on two harmonic oscillators.

<LDMCircuit />

**Strategy sets.** Island $j \in \{1,2\}$ declares a fleet $b_j \in S_j = [0,\infty)$ — the displacement amplitude of
the unitary $\hat D_j(b_j) = \exp(-i b_j \hat P_j)$.

**The clearing rule.** The expectation values at the end of the protocol are (LDM Eq. 11, Grau-Climent Eq. 5)

$$
b_1^{c} = b_1 \cosh\gamma + b_2 \sinh\gamma, \qquad b_2^{c} = b_2 \cosh\gamma + b_1 \sinh\gamma .
$$

Call $b_j^{c}$ the **licensed fleet** — the number of boats island $j$ is actually credited with. The total licensed
fleet is

$$
B^{c} = b_1^{c} + b_2^{c} = e^{\gamma}\,(b_1 + b_2).
$$

**Payoffs.** Let $v(B)$ be the value of a boat-trip when $B$ boats are on the water — decreasing in $B$, because boats
crowd each other out — and let $c$ be the cost of sending out one boat. Island $j$'s profit is

$$
u_j^{Q}(b_1, b_2) = b_j^{c}\,\big(v(B^{c}) - c\big).
$$

Read that carefully: $b_j^{c}$ multiplies the cost as well as the revenue (Grau-Climent Eq. 7). You are licensed on the
cleared number of boats, and you are billed on it too.

**Notation.** The dictionary against the fishery model in
[`common_pool.ipynb`](https://github.com/fretchen/fretchen.github.io/blob/main/notebooks/common_pool.ipynb), which the
[Moana fishing post](/blog/14/) is built on:

| here            | `common_pool.ipynb` | meaning                                   |
| --------------- | ------------------- | ----------------------------------------- |
| $b_j$           | `b_t[j]`            | boats island $j$ sends out — the decision |
| $B = b_1 + b_2$ | `total_boats_t`     | total fleet                               |
| $v(B)$          | $y(B)/B$            | value per boat                            |
| $b_j\,v(B)$     | `catch_players[j]`  | your catch, in money                      |
| $c\,b_j$        | `c_t = c0*b`        | cost of your fleet                        |
| $u_j$           | catch − cost        | profit                                    |

The one entry with no counterpart runs the other way: there is no fish **stock** anywhere in LDM. The crowding in $v(B)$
is all within a single round. Hold on to that; it is what §6 turns on.

**Preliminary observation.** $Q_\gamma$ is, at the level of its normal form, a _classical_ strategic game: deterministic
strategy sets and deterministic payoff functions $\mathbb{R}_+^2 \to \mathbb{R}$. The quantum apparatus (vacuum states,
$\hat J(\gamma)$, homodyne detection) is solely a physical _evaluation device_ for the map
$(b_1, b_2) \mapsto (u_1^Q, u_2^Q)$. Two strategic games with identical strategy sets and payoff functions are the same
game, whatever machinery computes the payoffs. The question "what is the classical equivalent?" is therefore well-posed
and purely algebraic.

## 2. Equivalence notions

We use two standard notions, in decreasing strength:

**Definition 1 (Strategic isomorphism up to positive scaling).** Games $G = (S_1, S_2; f_1, f_2)$ and
$G' = (S_1', S_2'; g_1, g_2)$ are strategically isomorphic up to positive scaling if there exist bijections
$\varphi_j : S_j \to S_j'$ and constants $\kappa_j > 0$ such that

$$
f_j(s_1, s_2) = \kappa_j \, g_j\big(\varphi_1(s_1), \varphi_2(s_2)\big) \quad \text{for all profiles } (s_1,s_2).
$$

Since expected-utility preferences are invariant under positive affine transformations, isomorphic games have identical
best-reply correspondences (under $\varphi$), identical Nash sets, identical correlated-equilibrium sets, and
equilibrium payoffs related by the constants $\kappa_j$.

**Definition 2 (Best-reply equivalence).** $G$ and $G'$ with common strategy sets are best-reply equivalent if
$\mathrm{BR}_j^{G} = \mathrm{BR}_j^{G'}$ for all $j$. This preserves Nash sets but not payoff levels.

## 3. Main result

Define the **co-liability fishery $C_\lambda$** for $\lambda \in [0,1)$: strategy sets $[0,\infty)$, ordinary profits
$u_j(b_1, b_2) = b_j\,\big(v(B) - c\big)$, and objectives

$$
V_j(b_1, b_2) = u_j(b_1, b_2) + \lambda\, u_{-j}(b_1, b_2).
$$

Each island maximises its own profit plus a fraction $\lambda$ of its neighbour's. This is the
_coefficient-of-cooperation_ family (Cyert & DeGroot 1973): $\lambda = 0$ is every island for itself, $\lambda \to 1$ is
joint profit maximisation.

**Proposition 1.** For every $\gamma \ge 0$ and every decreasing $v$, $Q_\gamma$ is strategically isomorphic up to
positive scaling to $C_\lambda$ with

$$
\boxed{\;\lambda = \tanh\gamma\;}
$$

via the relabeling $\varphi(b) = e^{\gamma} b$ (a bijection of $[0,\infty)$ onto itself) and the common constant
$\kappa(\gamma) = e^{-\gamma}\cosh\gamma = \tfrac{1}{2}\left(1 + e^{-2\gamma}\right) > 0$. Explicitly,

$$
u_j^{Q}(b_1, b_2) = \kappa(\gamma)\, V_j\!\left(e^{\gamma}b_1,\; e^{\gamma}b_2\right) \quad \text{for all } b_1, b_2 \ge 0.
$$

**Proof.** Write $t = \tanh\gamma$ and $\tilde b_j = e^{\gamma} b_j$. Then

$$
b_j^{c} \;=\; \cosh\gamma\,\big(b_j + t\, b_{-j}\big) \;=\; e^{-\gamma}\cosh\gamma\,\big(\tilde b_j + t\,\tilde b_{-j}\big),
$$

and $B^{c} = e^{\gamma}(b_1 + b_2) = \tilde b_1 + \tilde b_2 = \tilde B$. Hence

$$
\begin{aligned}
u_j^{Q} &= e^{-\gamma}\cosh\gamma \,\big(\tilde b_j + t\,\tilde b_{-j}\big)\big(v(\tilde B) - c\big) \\
&= \kappa(\gamma)\,\Big[\, \tilde b_j\big(v(\tilde B) - c\big) \;+\; t\,\tilde b_{-j}\big(v(\tilde B) - c\big) \,\Big] \\
&= \kappa(\gamma)\,\Big[\, u_j(\tilde b) + t\, u_{-j}(\tilde b) \,\Big] \;=\; \kappa(\gamma)\, V_j(\tilde b_1, \tilde b_2). \qquad \blacksquare
\end{aligned}
$$

The proof never touches the shape of $v$. It works because the value term $v(B^c) - c$ is a _common factor_: both
islands face the same crowding, so it survives the factorisation untouched. That is the whole trick, and it is worth
noticing how little the quantum machinery contributes to it.

Note also that this is an exact identity of payoff functions, not merely a first-order-condition match: every strategic
property of $Q_\gamma$ — best replies, equilibrium sets of every kind, comparative statics, even off-equilibrium
dynamics such as best-response adjustment — transfers verbatim.

**Verification against LDM's published results.** Specialise to linear value $v(B) = a - B$ and write $k = a - c$. Then
$u_j = b_j(k - B)$, and $C_\lambda$ is the Cournot duopoly with partial profit internalisation — this special case is
LDM's original model, and it is the only place in this post where the fishery is a firm. Its unique equilibrium solves
$k - 2b_j - (1+\lambda)\,b_{-j} = 0$, giving $b^{*} = \dfrac{k}{3+\lambda}$. Mapping back through $\varphi$, the
declared fleet is $e^{-\gamma}k/(3 + \tanh\gamma)$, and using
$3\cosh\gamma + \sinh\gamma = e^{-\gamma}\left(1 + 2e^{2\gamma}\right)$:

$$
b^{*}_{\text{declared}} = \frac{k \cosh\gamma}{1 + 2e^{2\gamma}},
$$

which is LDM Eq. (13). The equilibrium objective is
$V^{*} = b^{*}(1+\lambda)\big(k - 2b^{*}\big) = \dfrac{k^2 (1+\lambda)^2}{(3+\lambda)^2}$, so

$$
u^{Q*} = \kappa(\gamma)\, V^{*} = \frac{k^2\, e^{\gamma} \cosh\gamma}{\left(3\cosh\gamma + \sinh\gamma\right)^2},
$$

which is LDM Eq. (14), with the limits $k^2/9$ at $\gamma = 0$ ($\lambda = 0$: no cooperation) and $k^2/8$ as
$\gamma \to \infty$ ($\lambda \to 1$: the two islands act as one). The entire profit curve in LDM Fig. 2 is the
classical interpolation between own-profit and joint-profit maximisation, reparametrised by $\lambda = \tanh\gamma$.

## 4. What the mechanism is, classically

Proposition 1 says the protocol implements $\lambda = \tanh\gamma$. It does not say what that _is_. Here are four
classical objects it could be, in decreasing order of how exactly they match.

### (a) The harbourmaster's clearing rule

Factorise the clearing rule the way the proof does:

$$
b_j^{c} \;=\; \cosh\gamma\,\big(b_j + \tanh\gamma\; b_{-j}\big).
$$

Strip the common $\cosh\gamma$ — a change of units, applied identically to both islands — and what remains is a rule any
harbourmaster could publish and enforce:

> Your licensed fleet is your own declared boats, plus a fraction $\tanh\gamma$ of your neighbour's. You are billed and
> paid on that number.

No entanglement, no measurement, no light. Draw the protocol again with those labels and the picture does not change at
all:

Same two lines, same brackets, same read-out, same formula. This is the exact converse of the move in the
[prisoner's-dilemma post](/blog/31/), where a lawyer's filing cabinet turned out to be a quantum circuit. Here a quantum
circuit turns out to be a filing cabinet.

### (b) Mutual stakes in each other's fleet — exact, payoff-level

Let each island hold a silent stake $s$ in the other's catch: non-controlling in operations, but counted in the
objective. Island value satisfies the fixed point $\pi_j = u_j + s\,\pi_{-j}$, whose solution is

$$
\pi_j = \frac{u_j + s\, u_{-j}}{1 - s^2} = \cosh^2\!\gamma \,\big(u_j + s\, u_{-j}\big) \qquad \text{for } s = \tanh\gamma,
$$

using $\dfrac{1}{1-\tanh^2\gamma} = \cosh^2\gamma$. Combining with Proposition 1:

$$
u_j^{Q}(b) = \frac{e^{-\gamma}}{\cosh\gamma}\; \pi_j\!\left(e^{\gamma} b\right).
$$

So the realised "quantum profit" is proportional to island value in a classical fishery with reciprocal cross-ownership
$s = \tanh\gamma$ (Reynolds & Snapp, _Int. J. Ind. Org._ 1986). Maximal entanglement is full mutual ownership: the two
islands have merged.

### (c) An appropriation tax — best-reply equivalent

Let a regulator levy on island $j$ a per-boat tax at rate $\tau_j = \lambda\, b_{-j}$ — each island taxed in proportion
to the _other's_ fleet, which is the crowding externality it imposes — with revenue disposed lump-sum. Island $j$'s
objective becomes

$$
W_j = b_j\,\big(v(B) - c\big) - \lambda\, b_j\, b_{-j}.
$$

For linear $v$, the difference $V_j - W_j = \lambda\, b_{-j}\,\big(v(b_{-j}) - c\big)$ does not depend on $b_j$, hence

$$
\frac{\partial W_j}{\partial b_j} = \frac{\partial V_j}{\partial b_j}
$$

everywhere, so $C_\lambda$ and the taxed fishery are best-reply equivalent (Definition 2), with identical Nash sets.
This is the Pigouvian reading: the entangler internalises the crowding externality at fraction $\tanh\gamma$, a
regulator dialled continuously from absent ($\gamma = 0$) to fully internalising ($\gamma \to \infty$). Note the
hierarchy — reading (b) matches payoffs up to a positive constant, reading (c) matches only best replies, since realised
profits differ by the tax burden.

### (d) Conjectural variations

The first-order condition $k - 2b_j - (1+\lambda)\,b_{-j} = 0$ is also that of an island holding the conjecture
$\mathrm{d}b_{-j}/\mathrm{d}b_j = \lambda$: "if I send one more boat, my neighbour sends $\lambda$ more". This is the
textbook one-parameter bridge between competitive ($\lambda = -1$), Nash ($\lambda = 0$) and fully collusive
($\lambda = 1$) conduct; $\lambda$ is the conduct parameter of the empirical industrial-organisation literature. The LDM
protocol with $\gamma \ge 0$ reaches exactly the sub-interval $\lambda \in [0,1)$.

## 5. Robustness and scope

**Islands with different costs.** The four islands in the [Moana post](/blog/14/) have different costs per boat
(`c_islands` in the model). With $c_1 \ne c_2$ the factorisation breaks by a linear term:

$$
u_j^{Q}(b) = \kappa(\gamma)\,\Big[\, u_j(\tilde b) + t\, u_{-j}(\tilde b) + t\,\big(c_{-j} - c_j\big)\,\tilde b_{-j} \,\Big].
$$

Still an exact classical game — co-liability plus a per-boat transfer indexed to the neighbour's fleet — but no longer
pure cross-ownership. This demystifies the intricate "existence region" phenomenology reported in the asymmetric-cost
quantum-duopoly literature.

**$N$ islands.** With the symmetric multimode entangler

$$
\hat J(\gamma) = \exp\Big\{-\gamma \sum_{i<j} \big(\hat a_i^{\dagger}\hat a_j^{\dagger} - \hat a_i \hat a_j\big)\Big\}
$$

the same computation (diagonalising on the symmetric collective mode) yields licensed fleets affine in all $b_i$ and, by
the identical factorisation argument, objectives of the form

$$
V_j = u_j + \lambda(\gamma, N) \sum_{i \ne j} u_i
$$

— the $N$-island co-liability fishery, converging to the $N$-island cartel in the maximal-entanglement limit.

**Boundary of validity (the van Enk–Pike caveat).** The equivalence is a statement about the game as LDM define it, with
strategy sets restricted to displacements $S_j = \{\exp(-i b_j \hat P_j)\}$. If players are granted larger sets of
local operations — local squeezing before displacement, say, or displacements along $\hat X_j$ — the induced normal form
changes and Proposition 1 no longer describes it. But then LDM's own equilibrium analysis fails as well. Within the
minimal extension that constitutes the paper's model, the equivalence is exact and total.

## 6. Does it save the fishery?

Everything so far is a restatement. Now the question that motivated the whole exercise: put this institution on a real
fishery and does it stop the overfishing?

Take the model from [`common_pool.ipynb`](https://github.com/fretchen/fretchen.github.io/blob/main/notebooks/common_pool.ipynb),
which follows Leach's textbook treatment. A fleet of $B$ boats on a stock $s$ lands a total catch $y(B) = y_0\,s\sqrt{B}$,
shared in proportion to boats. So the value of a boat-trip is the average product,

$$
v(B) = \frac{y(B)}{B} = \frac{A}{\sqrt{B}}, \qquad A = y_0\,s,
$$

with a cost $c_0$ per boat. This is decreasing in $B$, so Proposition 1 applies with no quantum computation at all.
Maximising $V_j = (b_j + \lambda b_{-j})\big(A/\sqrt{B} - c_0\big)$ and imposing symmetry gives a closed form for the
total fleet:

$$
B(\lambda) = \left(\frac{A}{c_0}\right)^{2}\left(\frac{3-\lambda}{4}\right)^{2}.
$$

At the notebook's parameters — `y0=0.01`, `s=100`, `c0=0.125`, so $A/c_0 = 8$ — that gives:

|                                                                   | total boats |
| ----------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------- |
| open access (fishing until profits vanish)                        | 64.0        |
| $\gamma = 0$, $\lambda = 0$ — no cooperation                      | 36.0        |
| $\gamma = 1.15$, $\lambda = 9/11$ — 10 dB of squeezing            | 19.0        |
| $\gamma \to \infty$, $\lambda \to 1$ — the islands merge          | 16.0        |
| **sustainable fleet** (`b_sust`, where catch equals regeneration) | **7.29**    |

The entangler works, in its own terms. Dialling $\gamma$ from zero to infinity cuts the fleet from 36 boats to 16 — a
56% reduction, achieved with no regulator, no quota and no negotiation.

And it is not nearly enough. The sustainable fleet is 7.29 boats. Full entanglement overshoots it by more than a factor
of two.

Worse, this is not a near miss that a better protocol might close. Invert $B(\lambda)$ at the sustainable fleet:

$$
\lambda_{\text{needed}} \;=\; 3 - \frac{4\sqrt{B_{\text{sust}}}}{A/c_0} \;=\; 1.65 .
$$

LDM caps at $\lambda \to 1$. Sustainability here requires $\lambda = 1.65$ — the islands would have to weight their
neighbour's profit _more heavily than their own_, well past the point of merging into a single fishery.
**No amount of squeezing reaches it.** The ceiling is structural, not technological.

The reason is the missing row in the notation table of §1. The coefficient $\tanh\gamma$ internalises the externality
the two islands impose on _each other, within the round_ — crowding. It does nothing about the externality they jointly
impose on the _stock, across rounds_ — depletion. A cartel of two fishers still overfishes a commons; it simply
collects the rent instead of dissipating it. And a cartel is the very best this protocol can do.

This section goes beyond both LDM and Grau-Climent et al., neither of whom test the protocol against a regenerating
stock. I have not found it in the literature, but I have not finished looking — the open reading list is in my
[literature notes](/blog/32/).

## 7. What to make of it

**No quantum advantage in the game-theoretic sense.** Every solution-concept statement about $Q_\gamma$ is a statement
about the classical fishery $C_{\tanh\gamma}$. The cooperative outcome at $\gamma \to \infty$ is not a correlated
equilibrium of the original game being reached by quantum means; it is the Nash equilibrium of a _different_ classical
game, in which the externality was internalised by construction before anyone moved. This is the continuous-variable
instance of the general no-go: honest pre-play protocols cannot escape the correlated-equilibrium polytope of the
underlying game, and anything beyond it requires changing the game.

**The institution did not come for free.** In the prisoner's dilemma the entangler handed us Saul Goodman's filing
cabinet, and that felt like a gift. Here we can see the price. The referee who prepares
$\hat J(\gamma)\lvert 0\rangle\lvert 0\rangle$, applies $\hat J(\gamma)^{\dagger}$ and reads out the result _is_ the
harbourmaster: the coefficient $\tanh\gamma$ is hardwired into his procedure, and somebody has to run it, honestly, in
the middle of the game. Entanglement does not eliminate the regulator. It compiles the regulator into the physics of the
payoff channel. What is genuinely interesting about that is not game-theoretic but implementation-theoretic:
entanglement gives a tamper-evident physical carrier for a commitment device, with commitment strength set by a
laboratory parameter rather than by contract.

**So: can quantum game theory generate institutions?** For this problem, it generated one — and the honest verdict is
that it generated the wrong one. The mechanism the formalism happens to produce is co-liability, because that is what a
two-mode squeezer does to a pair of quadratures. What a commons actually needs is a cap tied to the stock. The
formalism had no way to know that, and no way to express it. Getting a mechanism out of the physics for free is only
useful if the physics is free to produce the mechanism you need.

**A footnote on how much squeezing you would need anyway.** Since $\lambda = \tanh\gamma$ and squeezing of $S$ decibels
means $e^{2\gamma} = 10^{S/10}$,

$$
\lambda(S) = \frac{10^{S/10}-1}{10^{S/10}+1},
$$

so 10 dB — around the state of the art for a stable optical setup — gives exactly $\lambda = 9/11 \approx 0.82$, and
even 20 dB only reaches $0.98$. The map $\gamma \mapsto \lambda$ compactifies $[0,\infty)$ onto $[0,1)$: realisable
squeezing buys conduct strictly between competition and merger, approaching but never attaining either end.
